FFT benchmark

Myla on a fast Fourier transform

Time per butterfly

A radix-2 FFT of n points does n/2 · log2 n butterflies: one complex multiply, one add and one subtract. Dividing the measured time by that count gives a figure that compares across sizes and across languages that ran different numbers of repetitions. The axis is logarithmic, so equal distances are equal ratios, and further left is faster.

Why Myla is where it is

At 4096 points Myla executes about five times the instructions of C at -O2 (1,839 million against 361 million for 200 round trips, counted with callgrind on the two-list version). The butterfly's disassembly shows where they go. Element accesses reload the list's pointer from self, compare the index with the length, and compute k * step again; intermediate values are kept on the stack between them. C# checks array bounds too, and still runs within 1.5 times of C.

In the struct version the complex multiply, add, subtract and conjugate are real function calls, about a third of that version's instructions. The C version writes that arithmetic out by hand, and in C# the struct with operators is the faster of its two versions.

At a million points every compiled version spends most of its time waiting for memory: the textbook loop strides through the table of factors and both halves of each block. That hides most of the difference in the code, which is why Myla lands next to C there.
