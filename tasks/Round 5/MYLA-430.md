Work package P16 of [[Project/Review-2026-11-09]] (wave B, after [[MYLA-415]]). M27: a checked 64-bit `*` divides to detect overflow, three to five times slower in hot loops.

- [x] The overflow flag (`jo`, `jc`; aarch64's flags; wasm32's wide multiply) instead of the division, through the IR's check
- [x] On the three back ends, with the overflow tests and the checked benchmarks before and after

## What was built

- **The IR: the intrinsic `fits op int64` / `fits op uint64`** ([[selfhost/ir.my]], `Intrinsic.Fits(op, signed)`): whether `a op b` (`+`, `-`, `*`) on 64-bit integers fits their type, a bool. In a checked build ([[MYLA-395]]'s `checked_arith`, [[selfhost/lower.my]]) a 64-bit `+`, `-` or `*` is now `%ok = intr fits * int64(%a, %b)`, `check %ok overflow`, then the plain `%d = * int64 %a, %b`: the operation reads its operands last, so the allocator can give its result an operand's register, as in a plain build. It replaces the signs' four instructions (`+`, `-`), the unsigned comparison and the call of std.rt's `mul_overflows_i64`/`mul_overflows_u64`, which divided; those two go from std.rt. The verifier checks the operator and that the operands are 64-bit integers of its signedness. Narrower widths keep the exact 64-bit operation and comparison, which costs what the flag would (an `int32` `+` stays `addq`, `movslq`, `cmpq`, `je`). [[Architecture/IR]] documents the intrinsic and the order.
- **x86-64** ([[selfhost/ir_x86.my]], `emit_checked_op`): the three instructions as one operation and `jno` (`jnc` for an unsigned `+` or `-`; `mulq`, whose high half sets OF, for an unsigned `*`) over the trap call. In place where the plain operation would be: in the result's register when it holds an operand already (as `emit_bin_in_reg`), or in a variable's register when only the store into it reads the result (`x += y`, as `emit_in_place`); else through %rax. The operands' second read by `fits` is uncounted, so a value just computed in %rax isn't stored for it. A `fits` on its own (no `check` right after) is the operation again and `setno`; with its `check` alone, the operation and the jump (`emit_fits_check`).
- **aarch64** ([[selfhost/ir_a64.my]]): `adds`/`subs` and `cset` on V (signed) or C (unsigned: `cc` for `+`, `cs` for `-`); `*` compares `smulh`'s high half with the low half's sign (`asr #63`), or `umulh`'s with 0.
- **wasm32** ([[selfhost/ir_wasm.my]], `wasm_fits`): no flags, so a signed sum is checked as "below `a` exactly when `b` is negative" (a difference: above `a`), an unsigned one against `a` (a difference: `a >= b`); a product of operands both in int32's range (unsigned: below 2^32) fits without more, the usual case in a loop; any other product fits when dividing it by `b` gives `a` back, `b` of 0 and -1 apart (the minimum by -1 overflows, and `div_s` would trap). The wasm32 the toolchain targets has no 64×64→128 multiply, so the division stays, for large operands only.
- **The hoisting of copy-on-write checks** ([[selfhost/hoist.my]]) treats `fits` as the computation it is, as it did the `bin`s it replaces; a loop with a checked 64-bit `*` no longer has a call in it, so it now qualifies (and the inliner copies such functions, which it didn't with the call).

## Decision (proposal, the owner decides)

**A new intrinsic before the operation, not a new instruction kind and not a pattern in each back end.** Built: `fits` as an `intr` (MYLA-264's place for "what every backend does in place"), giving the flag, and the ordinary `check` and `bin` around it, so the IR's model of D175 stays "plain IR, the same on every target": a backend that knows nothing better computes the flag on its own (x86-64 `setno`, aarch64 `cset`, wasm32 the tests), and one that does fuses the three. Alternatives: (1) an instruction with two results (`%d, %ok = *? int64 %a, %b`): exactly the hardware's shape, but every pass that lists a definition (`inst_defs`, `writes_temp`, the SSA renaming, the inliner, the allocator) would have to learn a second one; (2) pattern-matching the existing IR in each back end (the signs' five instructions, the call of `mul_overflows_i64`): no IR change, but three fragile matchers of a sequence any later pass may disturb, and the division would stay where a backend doesn't match; (3) a trapping `bin` (`+!` with a position, as a division traps): one instruction, but the check stops being a `check`, and every pass that reads a `bin`'s operator would need to know that one may trap.

## Evidence

- **Failing first**: `test_overflow`'s new `test_64_bit_checks_take_the_flags` (the checked code of a 64-bit `*`, unsigned `*`, `+` and unsigned `-` takes the flags: `imulq`/`mulq`/`addq`/`subq` with `jno`/`jnc` and no division on x86-64, `smulh`, `umulh`, `adds`, `subs` on aarch64, the 32-bit short cut on wasm32; no call of `mul_overflows`) failed at the base (`AssertionError: … std.rt's division by the operand is called`) and passes now, natively, on aarch64 (qemu) and wasm32 (Node). `test_64_bit_edges_trap_where_they_overflow` (1,128 cases: `+`, `-`, `*` of two operands, in place (`x op= b`) and with a constant (`a + 1`, `a * -1`, `a - (-2147483648)`, …) at the edges of int64 and uint64: 0, ±1, ±2, the int32 range's ends, 3037000499 and 3037000500 (around √2^63), ±2^62, the extremes; each must trap exactly where its value doesn't fit, checked, and wrap in a plain build, 256 runs that each end in their overflow) passed at the base too, as a guard, and passes now on the three targets. `ir.my`'s verifier cases for a `fits` of a pointer and of `/`.
- **The hot loop** (`a = a + i * b - (c - i)`, `c = (c * b + i) & 1048575`, `b = 3 - b`, `i += 1`, all `int64`, 3 million times), callgrind: plain 45.0 M instructions before and after; checked 456.0 M before (10.1 times plain), **69.0 M now (1.53 times plain: 23 instructions an iteration, the plain build's 15 and one `jno` per operation)**. Under qemu-aarch64 (median of 5, alternating): 0.544 s → 0.201 s; under Node (wasm32): 0.164 s → 0.088 s (Node's start included).
- **The benchmarks, checked** (callgrind at each `profile_size`, ASLR off, the least of 3 runs): the geometric mean of checked over plain **1.2341 → 1.1461** (+23.4% → +14.6%): string_build 143.0 M → 97.1 M (−32.1%), map_remove 18.87 M → 15.51 M (−17.8%), closures 163.0 M → 134.5 M (−17.5%), word_freq 164.4 M → 147.7 M (−10.2%), quicksort 111.5 M → 102.0 M (−8.5%), game_of_life −0.6%, the rest unchanged (they check `int32` arithmetic only). The calls were in the benchmarks' own `int64` arithmetic, their checksums and generators (string_build's `checksum`, map_remove's `key` and `slide`, word_freq's `text`, closures' lambda), and std.num's `parse_int` of the size. Under qemu-aarch64 (wall, median of 5) the checked geometric mean is 0.955 of the base's (string_build −16.8%, map_remove −9.2%, closures −8.7%); under Node 0.987, within Node's noise (its start dominates these sizes).
- **A plain build is unchanged**: the assembly of the 13 benchmarks and the hot loop, for x86-64, aarch64 and wasm32 (42 files), built by the base compiler with the base library and by the new compiler with the base library, is byte for byte the same; with the new library it differs only in 524 lines of position records (`.long line, col`), std.rt being 33 lines shorter. callgrind: plain builds within a dozen instructions of the base (fib 10,343,358 → 10,343,346), but ackermann, whose count varies by ±1.5% from run to run with ASLR off too.
- **A `--checked` compiler compiles itself** to the plain compiler's assembly byte for byte (gen_main, 3.4 s to build, 2.6 s to run); the bootstrap reaches its fixed point.
- **The checked parity programs**: every program of `test_compiler_parity` (207) built `--checked` prints and exits as its plain build does, natively, on aarch64 (qemu) and on wasm32 (Node): 0 differ on each (none overflows, as the interpreter, which traps, agrees with the plain build).
