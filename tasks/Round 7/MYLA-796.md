## What changed

- **M4, D258** (`selfhost/loader.my`, the loader change the package allows, named here; nothing in `selfhost/checker.my`): `Loader.without_unused_float` drops std.float from the implicit modules' files unless a module of the program, read as bytes by `may_call_method`, calls a method of one of its names (`float_method_names`: `.sqrt(`, `x . abs ()`, `.max<`), or has an impl on a float type (`impl float64`, so a program's own `sqrt` is still the duplicate naming std.float). Comments and strings count, as an f-string's holes hold calls: the test errs on the side of loading. A program that calls no such method loads, renames and registers neither std.float nor the std.math it imports; for one that does, the checker registers their methods as before. A name std.int shares (`abs`, `min`, `max`, `pow`, `clamp`, `signum`) loads it for an integer's call too. It is one call in `load_linked`, after the entry and its imports are loaded and before `load_implicit`.
- **M6** (`lib/std/math.my`, `lib/std/float.my`): std.math has fdlibm's `expm1`, `sinh`, `cosh`, `tanh` and `cbrt` (s_expm1.c, e_sinh.c, e_cosh.c, s_tanh.c, s_cbrt.c), and FreeBSD's `log10`: ln(1 + f) in two parts times 1/ln(10) in two, so the digits near 1 stay (the old one added k·log10(2) to log10 of the mantissa in [1, 2), which cancelled for x in (0.5, 1)). std.float's methods call them; its own `cbrt` (pow and a Newton step), `sinh`, `cosh` and `tanh` (from `exp`, a series near 0) are gone. `clamp` of a NaN is NaN at both widths (Low).
- **H11** (`scripts/gen_std_linalg.py`, and the six modules it writes): a `Matrix`'s shape is counted in int64. `shape_len` gives `zero_matrix`, `identity` and `Matrix * Matrix` a named runtime error for a negative side or more elements than a list holds; `matrix(r, c, data)` compares in int64 (`a 65536x65536 matrix takes 4294967296 elements, got 0`); `impl FromJson for Matrix` returns that as its error at `$.data` (a negative side at `$.rows` or `$.cols`). Nothing in `lib/std/json.my` changed: the decoder is the matrix module's impl.
- **M7**: `MatN.inverse` is the adjugate over the determinant (Mat4's from the 2x2 minors of its column pairs, no longer through `Matrix`'s elimination; `invert2` … `invert4`), `None` when the determinant is 0 or not finite or the inverse isn't finite. Then the matrix is scaled by two powers of two that bring its largest element into [1, 2) (`pow2_scale`, subnormals included) and inverted again, so a well-conditioned matrix of tiny or huge elements has its inverse; a matrix whose elements span the exponent range keeps the unscaled one. `std.matrix32` inverts in float64 and rounds. `MatN.is_finite()` is new. `Matrix.inverse` keeps its rule, a pivot of 0 (LAPACK's), now documented (see Proposals).
- **Low**: `Matrix.row`/`col` and `MatN.row` out of range, and `Vector`'s index (read and written), are named runtime errors (`column 3 out of range for a 2x3 matrix`, `index 5 out of range for a vector of 2`); `MatN[(r, c)]` names both; `zeros`/`filled` of a negative length too. `Format` of vectors, matrices and quaternions applies its spec to each element (`with_spec`, through a `T: Format` bound, so a spec that doesn't fit is the caller's runtime error as for a float). `length` (VecN, `Vector`, `Quat`) scales by the largest component where the squares' sum would over- or underflow, so `normalized()` of `(1e200, 1e200, 0)` is `(0.707…, 0.707…, 0)`; `angle_between` takes the dot of the normalized vectors. The linear algebra types read a JSON `null` component as NaN, which std.json writes for one, so a NaN vector decodes (a plain float doesn't: [[MYLA-851]]). [[MYLA-586]]'s position needs the map operations to report it: [[MYLA-850]]. [[MYLA-550]]'s note: D184 in the new modules is the shapes and ranges above. The "stale docs line" of [[MYLA-586]]'s row I didn't find (every page and the skill say floats are `Hash`); [[MYLA-850]] asks for `test_float_keys`' row in [[Development/Testing]] to change with the position.
- **M17**: filed as [[MYLA-849]], with the profile: reading each candidate digit count back with the big-integer parser is 99.9 % of it (`lib/std/rt.my` and `lib/std/num.my`, not this lane's files).
- **Cases**: written by the generator from Python's numbers (`num`, `num32`, `vec`, `mat`, `matmul`, `int_inverse` of integer matrices of determinant 1, the review's singular Mat4), so an expected value is computed, not copied; trigonometric ones in the module's precision (a float32 angle's cosine rounded to float32).

## Proposals

1. **When `inverse` is None** (M7, not settled by a decision). Built: the fixed sizes by the determinant, None when it is 0 or not finite (or the inverse isn't), the matrix scaled by a power of two where the determinant under- or overflows; `Matrix` by its pivots, None for a pivot of 0 (LAPACK's `getrf`, numpy's `inv`). An exactly singular matrix of small integers has a determinant of exactly 0, so it is None; one singular only by rounding still gets huge elements, as in glam and numpy. Alternatives: (a) a tolerance, None when a pivot (or the scaled matrix's determinant) is within n·ε of 0: catches the rounding cases too, but a badly scaled matrix that has an inverse (`diag(1e-20, 1)`) is then None unless its rows are equilibrated first, and the threshold is a choice no caller sees; (b) `Matrix` by its determinant too, which overflows for a large n.
2. **A NaN in JSON** ([[MYLA-851]]): the linear algebra types read `null` as NaN (built), as std.json writes one; plain floats don't. The alternatives are in that task.
3. **D258's form.** Built: the loader decides from the program's text, by the names of std.float's methods, before anything is checked. Alternative: the checker loads the module at the first float method it doesn't find, which is exact but needs the loader inside the checker (a module loaded mid-check, renamed and merged); the name test over-approximates only for an integer's `abs`, `min`, `max`, `pow`, `clamp`, `signum`, where it costs what every program paid before.
