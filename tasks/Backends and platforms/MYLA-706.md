A step of [[MYLA-165]], the Windows target, in the plan the owner accepted on 2026-11-14 ([[Project/Follow-up-plan#windows-2026-11-14]]). The reactor (D124) on I/O completion ports, through the platform interface; `std.net` over `ws2_32` (its seams are Windows-ready, [[Language/Standard-library/Net#porting-sockets-to-another-system]]).

- [x] Descriptor waits, timers and tasks on IOCP
- [x] TCP, UDP and name lookup; test_net and test_http pass under Wine (name lookup is std.net's own, with Windows' hosts file and name servers, not `GetAddrInfoW`: see the second proposal)

## What MYLA-703 left for this step

- The poller has timers only: `poller_new` gives a word in a cell of its own, `poller_wait` waits on it with `WaitOnAddress` for the nearest timer's time limit, and `poller_wake` (a frame pushed, a nearer timer) sets it and wakes the waiter with `WakeByAddressSingle` ([[lib/std/platform_windows.my]]). IOCP replaces the word: `PostQueuedCompletionStatus` is the wake.
- A read of the standard input that would suspend a task (`read_line_async`) blocks its worker; the other workers run the other tasks meanwhile.

## What changed

**The poller on an I/O completion port** ([[lib/std/platform_windows.my]], "the poller"; replacing D226's copy-and-wait). One port for the process (`net_port`); `poller_wait` is `GetQueuedCompletionStatusEx` on it with the nearest timer's time limit, `poller_wake` posts a packet of no operation (`PostQueuedCompletionStatus`, one at a time: `NET_WAKE`, as Linux's eventfd counts its wake-ups into one). Timers and tasks are as before.

*How completions become readiness.* std.rt asks for readiness, level-triggered and one-shot (D124, `poll_events`, MYLA-518); a port reports operations that ended. Each handle the port serves has a *watched handle* of std.platform's (`ws_*`, in a table by the handle's value) with an operation in flight that ends when the handle may have become ready, and a packet means only "look at this handle again". A watch arms the handle (its token and events) and posts it a packet; the waiting worker then looks (`ws_level`: `WSAPoll` with no wait, `PeekNamedPipe`, `WaitForSingleObject` with no wait, the operations' own status) and gives the event of a handle that is ready, disarming it, or starts the operation that ends when it may be (`ws_notify`) and keeps it armed. So a handle armed again while ready is ready at once, as std.rt's "level-triggered and fires once" needs, and a wake-up that comes early costs one look. The operations:
- a stream socket to receive from, a datagram socket, our end of a child's output pipe: a read of no bytes (`WSARecv`; `WSARecvFrom` with MSG_PEEK, as a datagram read into no room would be dropped; `ReadFile`), which ends when bytes are there, at the end or on an error, and takes none (checked under Wine: the bytes are still there after it);
- a listener: `AcceptEx` on a socket of its own (WSAIoctl's extension, so no mswsock), whose connection the next `sock_accept` takes before a plain `accept` would (its context set, `SO_UPDATE_ACCEPT_CONTEXT`);
- a connect of a non-blocking socket on the port: `ConnectEx` (bound to any address of its family first), its end the socket's room to write; `sock_error` gives how it ended and sets the connection's context;
- a send that would block (`WSAEWOULDBLOCK`): a copy of at most 256 KiB sent overlapped, its length given back as sent; until it ends the next send is WOULD_BLOCK, the socket not writable, and how it failed, if it did, is the next send's error;
- a child's end: a wait of the thread pool's (`RegisterWaitForSingleObject`) whose callback, the runtime's `__win_wait_posted` (a few lines of [[selfhost/runtime_x86.my]], as the pool's thread has no state block of the runtime's), posts the packet;
- what can't be waited for, a pipe not ours (an inherited standard input) and a datagram socket's room, armed, is looked at each millisecond (the wait's limit a millisecond while there is one, `NET_PEEKS`); the console and files stay ready at once, as on Linux a regular file is.

*Workers.* One worker waits at a time (std.rt's `__exec_polling`); the others watch, forget and wake. The net state (`__win_net`, [[selfhost/codegen.my]]) and the watched handles are under one spin lock, held for loads and stores and for calls that only start an operation, never one that waits (it yields its CPU after 64 spins). A wait holds no handle: it holds packets, which name their watched handle by the OVERLAPPED inside it, never by the handle's value, and a watched handle lives until no packet is due for it (`refs`). So a forget only disarms, and a handle can be closed while a wait goes on: its operations end cancelled and their packets find it closed (D226's copy and its wait for the forgetting worker are gone, and with them the `watches_closed` case it couldn't serve).

*A close while a send goes on.* Closing a socket (or our end of a pipe) cancels its operations, and with an overlapped send the bytes it took; Linux sends what a closed socket's buffer holds. So `sock_close` with a send going on hands the close to a wait of the thread pool's on that send's event (`ws_close_later`: `__win_wait_posted` calls `closesocket`), and the watched handle is freed once that has run. A socket closed after a 4,000,000-byte `write_async` reaches its reader whole; with the close done at once the reader got 3,919,872 bytes. A send still going when the program ends is lost (the process's I/O is cancelled with it).

*Without tasks* no poller exists and nothing would take the port's packets, so a program without tasks puts no handle on the port: everything is Winsock's plain non-blocking calls and `WSAPoll` (`sock_poll`), and `sock_poll` of a socket on the port waits on its operation's event where one goes on.

**Sockets over Winsock 2** ([[lib/std/platform_windows.my]], "sockets", D212). `WSAStartup` before the first socket, so a program that opens none doesn't import ws2_32 (the poller calls Winsock's functions through their addresses kept then, `ws2`, so a program with tasks and no socket doesn't either: `HelloWorld.test_an_import_library_per_dll` holds it). Every `sock_*` function of the platform interface: `WSASocketW` (overlapped, not inherited; an IPv6 socket takes IPv4 too, `IPV6_V6ONLY` off, as Linux's; a datagram socket's `SIO_UDP_CONNRESET` off, as an unconnected Linux socket ignores ICMP's "port unreachable"), `ioctlsocket(FIONBIO)`, `bind`, `listen`, `accept` (set back to blocking: Winsock's takes the listener's mode), `connect`, `SO_ERROR`, `send`, `recv`, `sendto`, `recvfrom` (`WSAEMSGSIZE` is the datagram cut, no error), `getsockname`, `getpeername`, `shutdown`, `TCP_NODELAY`, `SO_BROADCAST` (option 3, `SO_REUSEADDR`, does nothing, as the porting notes say), `WSAPoll`, `closesocket`. Windows' AF_INET6 is 23 in the `sockaddr`s (`sockaddr_of`, `record_into`). Winsock's errors are std.ioerror's causes (`wsa_cause`: WSAEWOULDBLOCK WOULD_BLOCK, WSAECONNREFUSED CONNECTION_REFUSED, …, the porting notes' list), and an overlapped operation's NTSTATUS too (`status_cause`). `SOCKETS` is true.

**Name lookup**: std.net's own resolver, as on every target, with Windows' places: `net_config(0)` is `%SystemRoot%\System32\drivers\etc\hosts`, `net_config(1)` the name servers of `GetNetworkParams` (iphlpapi) as `nameserver` lines. Under Wine: `C:\windows\System32\drivers\etc\hosts` and the host's `8.8.8.8:53`, `8.8.4.4:53`; `lookup("localhost")` gives `127.0.0.1 ::1`.

**A child's pipes** ([[lib/std/platform_windows.my]], `pipe_pair`): named pipes of this process's (`\\.\pipe\myla-<pid>-<n>`, one instance, no remote client), whose end here is overlapped (a watched handle, on the port in a program with tasks) and the child's plain, as a program expects its standard streams. A read of ours is overlapped with an event of its own whose low bit keeps the port out of it (`pipe_read`), a write is MYLA-726's. The pipe watching of D226 (each anonymous pipe looked at every millisecond) is gone: a task waiting for a child's output waits on the port. `run_with` and the closes go through `close`, which frees the watched handle.

**Tests.** [[tests/burn/test_windows.py]]'s `Sockets` (new): `SOCKETS`, 200 connections echoed through loopback (each a pair of tasks, every tenth message 300,000 bytes, past what a socket buffers) beside 50 children and an HTTP server and its client within the program, on 1, 4 and 16 workers; `CLOSED_WHILE_SENDING`; and MYLA-726's reproduction. `HelloWorld.test_a_socket_imports_winsock` (was `test_what_isnt_there_yet_says_so`, sockets `Unsupported`). [[scripts/windows-runner.py]]'s cases `net/echo` (test_net's ECHO), `net/http-one-program` (test_http's ONE_PROGRAM), `net/sockets-and-children`, `net/a-close-waits-for-its-send` and `process/input-while-output`, so CI's `windows` job runs them on Windows. `#test` cases for `wsa_cause`, `status_cause`, `ws_slot`, `poll_ready`. Excuses lifted (`tests/burn/_targets.py`): test_net's `Tcp`, `Udp`, `Dns`, `Errors` and `Primitives`, test_reactor_scaling's `RoundTrips`, `Accepts`, `Closed` and `Lots`, test_router's `Router`; test_net's `NetOnWasm` and test_http's `HttpOnWasm` run on wasm32 only, and test_http's socket classes run on Windows (its `NO_SOCKETS`). test_net's `test_the_configuration` checks the Windows hosts file's path on Windows (the interpreter is Linux's there).

## Proposals

1. **Windows' poller waits on an I/O completion port, its readiness a look that a packet asks for** (built). Proposed row, superseding D226: *"Windows' poller waits on one I/O completion port of the process's (`GetQueuedCompletionStatusEx`, a wake posted with `PostQueuedCompletionStatus`), and gives std.rt readiness, not completions ([[MYLA-706]], superseding D226): each armed handle has an operation going that ends when it may be ready (a read of no bytes, AcceptEx, ConnectEx, the send or write that would have waited, sent from a copy; for a child's end a wait of the thread pool's that posts a packet), and a packet only makes the poller look again and give the event, or start the operation again; a watched handle lives until no packet is due for it, and a close while a send or a write goes on is done once it has ended."* Alternatives: `AFD_POLL` on the port (what libuv, mio and wepoll use: readiness itself, one operation a socket, but through ntdll's `NtDeviceIoControlFile`, which D212 leaves out); `ProcessSocketNotifications` (Windows 10 21H1 on, readiness through the port; Wine 9.0 doesn't have it, so nothing here could test it, and older Windows lacks it); `WSAPoll` beside a wake-up socket (the socket set copied and scanned each wait, no pipes or processes, and WSAPoll misses a failed connect before Windows 10 2004).
2. **Names are looked up by std.net itself on Windows too** (built): the hosts file under `%SystemRoot%` and the name servers of `GetNetworkParams` (`net_config`), as the porting notes said, so `lookup`, `Resolver.lookup` and a resolver given by hand answer alike on every target. Alternative: `GetAddrInfoW` (the system's resolver: its order of hosts, DNS, LLMNR and the rest, its cache and its DNS over HTTPS), which needs a primitive of its own (`__net_lookup`) on every target, the interpreter's included and so a `Stage:` commit, and gives `lookup` other answers than `Resolver.lookup` on Windows only. Proposed row: *"On Windows std.net looks names up itself, as on every target: the hosts file `%SystemRoot%\System32\drivers\etc\hosts` and the name servers `GetNetworkParams` gives ([[MYLA-706]]); `GetAddrInfoW` would need a primitive on every target."*
3. **A child's pipes are named pipes, ours overlapped, the child's plain** (built): what an I/O completion port needs, as Rust's and libuv's are. Handed on to another child (`stdin_from_stdout`), ours stays overlapped: Wine's child reads it (test_windows' `Children` pipeline); Windows' plain `ReadFile` on an overlapped handle waits on the handle, which CI's `windows` job shows (`process/std-process`). Alternative: anonymous pipes looked at every millisecond (D226's), which can't give MYLA-726 its wait.
4. **A send that would block takes a copy of at most 256 KiB, sent overlapped** (built; the next is WOULD_BLOCK until it ends, its failure the next send's error): Windows has no way to wait for a socket's room but an operation that needs it. Alternative: a send of no bytes as the probe, which Windows completes at once whatever the room.
